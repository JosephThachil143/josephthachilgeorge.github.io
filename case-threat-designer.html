Threat Designer

An agentic AI threat-modelling platform on Amazon Bedrock that let a four-person security team support 100+ product teams — making thorough threat modelling fast enough to be mandatory for every new service.

The challenge

TUI's security engineering team had four engineers supporting over 100 digital product teams. Each threat model took 4–6 hours of manual work — reviewing architecture diagrams, mapping threats to internal standards with STRIDE, researching issues, and documenting findings in Confluence. The team could only complete 3–4 sessions a month, so most teams waited weeks and threat modelling stayed a luxury rather than a default.

The goal: make thorough threat modelling fast enough that it could be mandatory for every new service — not reserved for the highest-priority projects.

The approach

We built an agentic AI workflow on Amazon Bedrock (Claude Sonnet 4.5), developed over a 10-week proof of concept with the AWS Generative AI Innovation Center, and taken to production within 30 days. The design went through several iterations — each failure pointing to the next improvement.

  • Metadata-driven standards selection Loading all 192 TUI security standards (338 KB) broke token limits. Instead, lightweight metadata is generated per document, and the model selects only the ~16 most relevant standards per architecture — cutting context from 338 KB to 21 KB (a 94% reduction) while keeping coverage.
  • Self-correcting threat generation A LangGraph agent uses a Reason-and-Act loop: it generates STRIDE threats, reviews its own output, runs gap analysis, and fills what's missing — mimicking how a human expert works. A validation node enforces full STRIDE coverage before finishing.
  • Web search for live threat intelligence For each technology in the architecture, the workflow queries authoritative sources (OWASP, NIST, CWE), with an S3 cache (7-day TTL) achieving a 70–80% hit rate. Only component names leave the system — never architecture details or customer data.
  • Human-in-the-loop validation Engineers can upload their own manual assessment; the system matches it against the AI output by semantic similarity and flags where severity ratings disagree — calibrating the AI's risk scoring over time.

Architecture

A serverless architecture in eu-central-1 (Frankfurt), keeping data within AWS to meet residency and security requirements. The agentic workflow runs on Amazon Bedrock AgentCore, which hosts long-running sessions with per-session isolation.

My role

I led the development of Threat Designer end-to-end and drove its adoption across the domain: integrating Gemini AI web search with the GenAI Lab and AWS GenAI team, delivering the V1.1 release, building features such as SSO, multi-user sharing, XLS export and an automated knowledge-base sync aligned to TUI Technology Standards. I also led 13 threat-modelling sessions with the tool and trained 8+ teams on AI-assisted threat modelling.

Results

  • Time per assessment reduced by up to 70% (from 4–6 hours).
  • Monthly throughput tripled — from 3–4 to 10–12 sessions (a 300% increase).
  • Security standards applied with 100% consistency, versus varying by engineer before.
  • 40–60 threats identified per architecture (vs 30–40 manually).
  • Adoption grew to 255 registered users, 86 threat models and 43 active creators — making threat modelling feasible as a mandatory step at scale.
